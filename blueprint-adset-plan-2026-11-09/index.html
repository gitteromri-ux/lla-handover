Why the "high intent" sets are not high intent

1. The audiences inside #108810 and #108814 are mostly empty. Live sizes from the API: every retargeting pool (Website visitors 30d, IC 30/90d, ATC 30/90d, Leads 30/90d, Purchase 30d, "IC30 OR ATC14") and every lookalike built from them reports under 1,000 people, which is Meta's floor for "too small to serve". The only audiences that actually deliver are three lookalikes: LLA 1% seed-382 (2.3–2.7M), Checkout-starters 180d LAL 2% (4.7–5.5M) and Julie CRM buyers LAL 2% (4.6–5.4M). A 2% lookalike of 101 buyers is 5M people modelled from 101 names: that is a cold audience with a warm label.
2. The purchase signal is the wrong product. Optimisation is PURCHASE, any product. In the last 30 days the pixel's purchases are ~$49 Masterclass buyers: 85% women, 40–53% aged 65+. Meta is therefore hunting Masterclass-buyer lookalikes for a $755 course. Blueprint has close to zero purchases in the window, so no set has a Blueprint signal to learn from.
3. The cost is the 65+ women on Reels, not the audiences. 30-day CPM in the purchase-optimised sets: women 45–54 $570–615, women 55–64 $619–711, women 65+ $683–842; men 55–64 $401–443, men 65+ $443–525. #108814 spent 53% on 65+ and 85% on women, hence $633.
4. #108810 and #108814 run the same audiences in two campaigns (seed-382 LAL, Julie LAL 2% v2, CRM buyers 101). They bid against each other. Pause #108810 (25–65+, 18 stacked audiences, $491 CPM, 0 ATC) and let one controlled set carry the LAL budget.
5. #108808 is cheap because it is low intent. $24 CPM, 526 link clicks, 406 LP views, 0 add-to-cart. 95% Reels, 87% women, 40% 65+. These are curiosity taps, not buyers; its KPI must be ATC rate, not CPC.
